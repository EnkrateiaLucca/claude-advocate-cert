# Lesson 08: Exam Strategy and Study Plan

**Covers: all seven domains** · Time in session: about 5 minutes in Segment 4 (exam-day strategy) and 10 minutes in Segment 5 (study plan and self-assessment)

This lesson has four parts: how to work through the exam in 120 minutes, how to register and avoid losing your fee, a four-week study plan, and a self-assessment rubric for all 30 exam objectives.

---

## 1. The exam at a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Exam code | CCAO-F |
| Items | 60, multiple-choice and multiple-response. Each item states how many answers to select |
| Time | 120 minutes. Plan for about 135 minutes of seat time with check-in and a short survey |
| Passing score | Scaled score of 720 on a 100–1,000 scale |
| Fee | $99 USD per attempt (partner-tier discounts may apply) |
| Delivery | Pearson VUE, online proctored or at a test center |
| Rules | Closed book, English only. No notes, phones, AI tools, second monitors, or browser translation tools |
| Results | Pass/fail and scaled score on screen at the end, plus percent correct by domain |
| Validity | 12 months. On-time renewal is a free, non-proctored assessment on the Partner Academy |

**Eligibility:** registration currently requires a company email on a recognized Claude Partner Network domain. Personal email addresses do not work. If your company is not a partner, the skills in this course still apply to your daily work, and you can check the Partner Academy for changes.

**About the score:** 720 is a scaled score. It does not mean 72% correct. Anthropic does not publish the conversion from raw to scaled score, and the domain percentages on your report do not decide pass or fail.

## 2. Exam-day time budget

You have 2 minutes per item on average. Most items take less. Use three passes.

| Pass | Clock | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Pass 1** | 0–80 min | Answer every item in order. If an item takes more than about 2 minutes, pick your best answer, flag it, and move on. Never leave an item blank |
| **Pass 2** | 80–110 min | Return to flagged items. Reread the stem for the deciding constraint. Change an answer only when you can name the reason |
| **Final sweep** | 110–120 min | Confirm no item is unanswered and every multiple-response item has exactly the number of selections it asks for |

Pacing checkpoints for Pass 1:

| Item | Clock |
|---|---|
| 15 | about 20 min |
| 30 | about 40 min |
| 45 | about 60 min |
| 60 | about 80 min |

Use the flag-for-review feature if the exam interface offers it. If you fall behind a checkpoint, speed up on the next few items instead of rushing the last ten.

## 3. How to read an item

1. **Read the question line first.** "Most appropriate action", "best first step", and "Choose TWO" change what you are looking for.
2. **Find the deciding constraint in the stem.** Look for words about privacy (names, accounts, PII), stakes (customer, regulator, legal, health), cost or volume (hundreds, routine, fast), recurrence (every week, same format), or recency (last month, current).
3. **Predict the answer before you read the options.** Use the core logic: verify specifics against the source, match the model or feature to the task, anonymize before upload, put recurring context in a Project, diagnose what changed, and keep a qualified human on high-stakes decisions.
4. **Eliminate by pattern** (next section).
5. **Choose the proportionate step that lets the work continue safely.**

---

## 4. Elimination tactics

Most wrong options fall into five patterns. Cross them out first.

| Pattern | What it sounds like | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Blind trust** | "Send it as-is, since Claude was confident." "It cited a source, so it's verified." | Confidence and polish are not evidence |
| **Fake safeguard** | "Tell Claude not to retain the data." "Ask Claude to rate its confidence." "Add a disclaimer." "Use Incognito." | It looks like a control but changes nothing about the risk |
| **Irrelevant fix** | "Reword it more formally." "Switch to a different AI platform." "Put the rule in the Project description." | It solves a problem the stem doesn't have |
| **Over-reaction** | "Skip the analysis." "Ban AI use." "Rebuild from scratch." | A proportionate path exists |
| **Over-engineering** | "Use the most capable model for everything." "Escalate every request to Legal." "Fully automate it on day one." | It ignores cost, speed, or the stated scope |

Three tie-breakers when two options survive:

- **Pick the option a careful professional could defend to the risk owner.**
- **Prefer fixing the cause** (the knowledge file, the instructions, the data) over working around it in every chat.
- **Distrust absolutes.** "Always", "never", "guaranteed", and "fully automatic" are usually wrong.

---

## 5. Multiple-response tactics

- **Read the count first.** The item tells you how many to choose. Select exactly that many.
- **Judge each option on its own.** Ask "Is this true, and does it answer this stem?" for every option.
- **Treat every multiple-response item as all-or-nothing.** No official source confirms partial credit. Prepare as if one wrong selection costs the whole item.
- **Watch for near-duplicates.** Two options that say the same thing in different words rarely both belong in the answer.
- **Reject the "partly right" option.** An option that is true in general but does not address the scenario is wrong here.

Community guides estimate that about a quarter of items are multiple-response. That figure is unofficial.

## 6. Registration, ID, rescheduling, and retakes

**Register and schedule**

1. Sign in to the Anthropic Partner Academy with your partner company email.
2. Register for the exam and pay by credit card.
3. Schedule through Pearson VUE, online or at a test center: https://www.pearsonvue.com/us/en/anthropic.html
4. Need accommodations? Request them through Pearson VUE and get approval before you schedule. Allow 10 days or more.

**ID**

- Bring valid, unexpired, government-issued photo ID.
- The name must match your registration exactly. To fix a mismatch, email certifications-support@anthropic.com at least 24 hours before your exam with the subject "Name Correction Request". Processing takes 24–48 business hours, so do it early. A mismatch at check-in means you cannot test and you lose the fee.

**Rescheduling and cancelling: use 48 hours as your rule**

- The exam guide says 24 hours. The Partner Academy FAQ and policies page say 48 hours. Reschedule or cancel at least **48 hours** before your appointment to stay safe.
- Cancelling in Pearson VUE does not refund you automatically. Email certifications-support@anthropic.com for the refund.
- A no-show or late arrival forfeits the fee.

**Online proctoring checklist**

- Run the Pearson system test on the computer and network you will use.
- Clear your desk of notes, books, phones, smart watches, headphones, and second monitors.
- Stay in webcam view for the whole session. Do not talk to anyone.
- Unscheduled breaks are not allowed unless approved in advance.
- You will accept an NDA before the exam. Declining ends the session with no refund. Do not copy, photograph, or share exam content afterward.

**Retakes**

- Waiting periods grow with each failed attempt: **14 days** after the first, **30 days** after the second, **90 days** after the third.
- Maximum **4 attempts** per exam in a rolling 12-month period.
- Each attempt costs the exam fee.

**Renewal**

The credential lasts 12 months. Renew on time with a free, non-proctored assessment on the Partner Academy. If it lapses, you retake the full exam at full price.

**When you review a miss**, label it with one of three causes:

- **Knowledge:** you didn't know a fact (for example, that Claude does not see the Project description).
- **Reading:** you missed the deciding constraint or the "Choose TWO".
- **Judgment:** you knew the facts and still chose an over-reaction or a fake safeguard.

Knowledge misses need the lesson again. Reading misses need slower stems. Judgment misses need more practice with the distractor patterns.

### Turn scores into priorities

1. Average your scores for each domain.
2. Compute a priority number: **(3 − domain average) × domain weight**.
3. Study the highest priority numbers first.

Example: D2 average 1.5 gives (3 − 1.5) × 21 = 31.5. D7 average 1.0 gives (3 − 1.0) × 10 = 20. D2 comes first even though your D7 score is lower, because D2 carries twice the weight.

**Ready to book?** Aim for at least 2 on every objective and 3 on most of Domain 2. Your practice-question score should also hold steady across two attempts spaced a week apart.

## 9. Exercise: self-assessment (in session)

**Goal:** Leave the session with a scored rubric and a draft study plan you have checked yourself.

**Steps**

1. **Score yourself (4 minutes).** Fill in the Score column for all 30 objectives. Be strict. A 3 means you could teach it.
2. **Calculate priorities (2 minutes).** Use the formula above and circle your top two domains.
3. **Draft a plan with Claude (3 minutes).** Open a new chat and paste your scores into this prompt:

```
I'm preparing for the Claude Certified Associate – Foundations exam (CCAO-F). Domain weights: D1 14%, D2 21%, D3 12%, D4 16%, D5 12%, D6 15%, D7 10%.

My self-assessment scores (0 = can't explain, 3 = do it routinely):
D1: [scores for 1.1–1.4]
D2: [scores for 2.1–2.6]
D3: [scores for 3.1–3.4]
D4: [scores for 4.1–4.5]
D5: [scores for 5.1–5.4]
D6: [scores for 6.1–6.4]
D7: [scores for 7.1–7.3]

I can study [N] minutes on weekdays and [N] minutes on weekends for four weeks.

Build a four-week plan as a table (week, day, focus objective, activity). Rank domains by (3 minus my average score) times the domain weight, and show that calculation first. Give my lowest-scoring objectives a hands-on task in claude.ai in addition to reading.
```

4. **Check the plan (1 minute).** Recompute one priority number yourself and compare it with Claude's. Make sure Domain 2 gets real time unless you scored it high. If Claude's arithmetic is off, correct it before you adopt the plan. This step is the same discernment habit the exam tests.

**Debrief:** the rubric covers the exam guide's first preparation step: "Study the exam blueprint in Section 6 and self-assess against each objective." Rescore at the end of Week 3 to see what moved.
